State, memory and execution

The legacy dense MDP has S = (C/q + 1)K states, three actions and a transition tensor (S, 3, S). Price transitions are exogenous; feasible actions deterministically move SOC and distribute probability over the next price bin. Infeasible actions carry -inf reward and are masked. At default C=500, q=100 and K=4, S=24. voltrl.py:374–481

The benchmark stores (24,K,K) hour-aware transitions or (K,K) price-only transitions, and finite-horizon action decisions (H,N_soc,K) as int8. Backward recursion retains two-dimensional value slices. No continuous-state approximation, neural network or rainflow cycle counter is implemented. voltrl_benchmark.py:78–102 voltrl_benchmark.py:173–224 voltrl_benchmark.py:311–397

voltrl.py:113–165

BatterySpec.degradation_cost(self, action: int | Action, soc_before_mwh: float | None=None) -> float

Return the stage degradation cost.

The legacy model is linear in internal energy throughput. The revised model retains a small linear component and adds a convex cumulative depth-of-discharge (DOD) potential on discharge plus an hourly extreme- SOC stress term. The DOD potential is normalized so a full 0-100-0% cycle has the same nominal cost as the legacy linear proxy, while shallow cycles are cheaper and deeper excursions are progressively more expensive.

select_bin_count_bic

voltrl.py:310–370

select_bin_count_bic(prices: Sequence[float], candidates: Iterable[int]=range(4, 11), validation_fraction: float=0.2, prior_strength: float=1.0) -> tuple[int, pd.DataFrame]

Select a quantile-bin count using chronological validation and BIC.

For each candidate, the first-order Markov kernel is fitted on the early training segment and scored on the held-out tail. To make likelihoods comparable across different bin counts, the categorical transition probability is converted to a piecewise-uniform conditional density by dividing by the destination-bin width. The criterion is BIC = -2 log L_density + (k(k-1) + k-1) log(n_validation_transitions); the final term counts transition and interior-edge parameters.

3. Build a finite-horizon native policy

import numpy as np
from voltrl import BatterySpec, generate_synthetic_prices
from voltrl_benchmark import (
    chronological_split, fit_state_model, finite_horizon_policy,
    simulate_finite_policy, trajectory_metrics,

train, test = chronological_split(frame)
battery = BatterySpec(
    charge_efficiency=.95, discharge_efficiency=.95,
    degradation_cost_per_mwh=5, nonlinear_degradation=True,
    dod_stress_exponent=1.6, linear_degradation_fraction=.25,
    soc_stress_cost_per_hour=1,

model = fit_state_model(train, n_bins=4, calendar_aware=True)

1. Energy grid, feasible actions and market cash

Let capacity be C MWh, internal power step P MW and interval Δt h. The internal energy step is q=PΔt. The code requires C/q to be an integer and uses E in {0,q,…,C}. Actions encode charge=0, idle=1, discharge=2, but their energy changes are +q, 0, −q. An action is feasible only when 0≤E+δ≤C. At default C=500 and q=100, the six states are 0,100,…,500 MWh. voltrl.py:36–45 voltrl.py:52–111 voltrl.py:412–481

Grid purchase and sale energies are

Ebuy=max(δ,0)ηc,Esell=ηdmax(−δ,0).E^{buy}=\frac{\max(\delta,0)}{\eta_c},\qquad E^{sell}=\eta_d\max(-\delta,0).

The cash flow is p(Esell−Ebuy)p(E^{sell}-E^{buy}); net reward subtracts the modeled degradation/stress cost. Both efficiencies act on grid energy, while the SOC step stays q. Thus a 100 MWh internal charge with ηc=.95 purchases 105.263158 MWh. max_power_mw is not a separately enforced 100 MW grid-import cap in this implementation. One charge followed by a discharge transfers the same internal q and returns ηcηd=.9025 of the purchased energy when both efficiencies are .95. voltrl.py:167–194

2. Aging proxy

Legacy linear aging is D=d|δ|D=d|\delta|. In nonlinear mode, with x=E/C, x′=(E+δ)/C, ν≥1, 0≤α≤1 and stress coefficient s:

D=αd|δ|+2(1−α)dC𝟏δ<0max{(1−x′)ν−(1−x)ν,0}+sΔt(x′−0.50.5)2.D=\alpha d|\delta|+2(1-\alpha)dC\,\mathbf{1}_{\delta<0}\max\{(1-x')^\nu-(1-x)^\nu,0\}+s\Delta t\left(\frac{x'-0.5}{0.5}\right)^2.

The nonlinear term is an increment of an absolute-SOC depth potential on discharge. It has no cycle history, rainflow counting, changing physical capacity or calibrated electrochemical damage state. The quadratic term uses after-action SOC and also charges idle actions away from mid-SOC. The primary specification uses d=5, α=.25, ν=1.6, s=1. voltrl.py:113–165 voltrl_benchmark.py:1671–1682

For a complete 0→C→0 traversal, the linear contribution is 2αdC. Discharge potential increments telescope to 2(1−α)dC. Their sum is 2dC; SOC stress is additional, so full-cycle normalization does not imply the all-in cost is exactly 2dC when s>0. The existing normalization test sets the default s=0. tests/test_voltrl.py:92–118

At E=200 MWh, idle costs 1((0.4−0.5)/0.5)2=0.041((0.4-0.5)/0.5)^2=0.04 currency/hour. The primary six-point grid omits exactly 250 MWh, so there is no grid point with zero quadratic stress. This explains the stored idle annualization of −350.4 (=−.04×8760). results_revision2/synthetic_summary.csv:1–7

3. Price discretization and transition inference

Quantile edges are fitted on training prices only. Edges must remain distinct, with at least 10K training observations; the outer transform bins extend to ±∞. Each bin's representative is its training mean. voltrl.py:217–266

For destination marginal m and transition counts Nij, smoothing is

Pij=Nij+λmj∑kNik+λ.P_{ij}=\frac{N_{ij}+\lambda m_j}{\sum_kN_{ik}+\lambda}.

The legacy estimator uses a smoothed marginal of destination observations and λ=1 by default. The benchmark uses its fitted frame's bin marginal, λ=12, and optionally a separate count matrix per current UTC hour h. Smoothing is validated as finite/nonnegative; λ=0 fails when a needed row has no outgoing observations. Price transitions are exogenous to battery actions. voltrl.py:278–307 voltrl_benchmark.py:173–224

The benchmark scores the next observed price with a full-support mixture

f(pt+1∣zt,ht)=∑jPht,zt,j𝒩(pt+1;μj,σj2).f(p_{t+1}\mid z_t,h_t)=\sum_j P_{h_t,z_t,j}\,\mathcal{N}(p_{t+1};\mu_j,\sigma_j^2).

Each σj is at least max(.5,.05×pooled training sample SD). The score is mean negative log density using a log-sum-exp computation. Three expanding training folds use boundaries at 1/2, 2/3, 5/6 and all of the development window. K minimizes mean hour-aware validation NLL; the same K is then used for the price-only ablation. 4. Stationary and finite-horizon control

The legacy stationary Bellman update is

Vn+1(s)=maxa∈A(s)[R(s,a)+γ∑s′P(s′∣s,a)Vn(s′)],0≤γ<1.V_{n+1}(s)=\max_{a\in A(s)}\left[R(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)V_n(s')\right],\quad0\le\gamma<1.

VI starts at zero and stops when max absolute value change is ≤tolerance, subject to the iteration cap. PI starts with idle, evaluates a fixed policy via (I−γPπ)V=rπ, and improves it until actions stop changing. The reported Bellman residual is ∥TV−V∥∞\|TV-V\|_\infty. For this discounted finite-state system, the contraction argument bounds value error by residual/(1−γ); small residual is numerical evidence under the model, not physical validation. voltrl.py:494–588

The benchmark instead computes a finite-horizon recurrence, with terminal salvage VH(E,z)=vηdEV_H(E,z)=v\eta_dE and v equal to the training-median price:

Vt(E,z)=maxa∈A(E)[R(E,μz,a)+γ∑jPht,z,jVt+1(E+δ(a),j)].V_t(E,z)=\max_{a\in A(E)}\left[R(E,\mu_z,a)+\gamma\sum_jP_{h_t,z,j}V_{t+1}(E+\delta(a),j)\right].

The primary γ=1 needs no infinite-horizon convergence test; a finite backward loop evaluates each time slice. The observed price determines the current bin when applying the policy. The hour-aware model conditions transitions on UTC hour; the price-only model omits that conditioning. Finite planner ties prefer idle, charge, then discharge; legacy argmax order starts with charge. voltrl_benchmark.py:72–74 voltrl_benchmark.py:311–469

The oracle runs backward on the entire actual test-price sequence with the same SOC grid, costs and terminal salvage. It is an upper bound for feasible policies evaluated under that same finite objective, not an implementable policy input. Discounted stationary policies can optimize a different objective from the undiscounted comparison, and the documentation keeps those cases separate. voltrl.py:648–714

5. SARX forecasts and the historical block

The fitted regression standardizes prices and uses an intercept, lags 1,2,24,48,168 and sine/cosine pairs for daily, weekly and annual periods. Ridge λ=.001 penalizes all coefficients except the intercept. Forecasts recurse from available history and are clipped to training .001/.999 quantiles extended by 25% of their range. This is a fixed training fit, not online retraining. voltrl_benchmark.py:517–652

Synthetic MPC uses the current observed price plus up to 23 future forecasts, solves a forecast DP, and applies its first action. Historical dispatch forecasts 24 hours from the final available prior-delivery-hour observation, fixes the whole action vector, carries SOC between days, and settles against actual prices afterward. Seasonal-mean and prior-day persistence forecasts are explicit historical baselines. No exchange auction calendar is implemented. voltrl_benchmark.py:655–811

For a trajectory, cash profit is Σr, adjusted profit is J=∑trt+vηd(EH−E0)J=\sum_t r_t+v\eta_d(E_H-E_0), annualized adjusted profit is 8760J/H8760J/H for H hourly observations, and equivalent full cycles are ∑t|δt|/(2C)\sum_t|\delta_t|/(2C).
